# Artist’s statement and residency intentions

Applicant: Jonathan Carroll
Status: First draft for review. Intended for a maximum one-page PDF; final pagination not yet checked.
Working project title: Creighton Stations

## Draft text

My interdisciplinary practice brings sculpture, sound, performance and interactive technologies into conversation with family archives and industrial landscapes. I build situations in which objects do more than illustrate a story: they listen, accumulate traces and perform. Working with salvaged materials, recordings and spatial narratives, I ask how environmental harm persists in bodies, memories and places after official accounts declare a site restored. My ongoing research centres on Sydney’s former steelworks and Tar Ponds and my family’s relationship to industrial labour and illness. Rather than claiming a single explanation for these inheritances, I make their uncertainties audible and tangible.

At Est-Nord-Est, I propose to continue developing Creighton Stations: site-responsive sculptural listening devices that collect and replay fragments of their surroundings. The project takes the recording technologies and selective practices of folkloric collection as a point of departure, asking what a contemporary collection device might preserve, miss or transform. Built from salvaged cabinetry, medical shipping boxes and found hardware, the stations house microphones, microcontrollers, storage and speakers. Sound-triggered recording and intermittent playback allow each object to become both an archive and a performer. Image transfers and QR-linked spatial narratives connect its physical surface to further layers of sound, text and memory.

I would use the residency to test how these devices respond to a place beyond the project’s existing Nova Scotia sites, without treating distinct local histories as interchangeable. Walking, listening and attending to the immediate environment would inform experiments with enclosures, recording thresholds, playback intervals and relationships between multiple stations. I want to investigate when a station appears to document a place and when its own behaviour changes the situation it records. Studio tests would explore how accumulated sounds become an installation in which objects listen and respond to one another. The aim is a refined group of prototypes and a clearer method for future site-specific installations, rather than a predetermined finished exhibition.

Any recording involving participants would be developed through explicit invitation and consent, with clear information about recording and playback. Outdoor tests would depend on permission and suitable conditions; studio-based testing remains an alternative. I would bring the core electronics and software. Anticipated technical needs are a workbench, electricity, basic woodworking and assembly tools, and space for controlled sound tests, subject to confirmation with the residency. Specialist fabrication, equipment loans and outdoor installation access are not assumed.
