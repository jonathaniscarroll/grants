# Artist’s statement and residency intentions

Applicant: Jonathan Carroll
Status: Revised draft using the artist’s published CV. Intended for a maximum one-page PDF; final pagination not yet checked.
Working project title: Creighton Stations

## Draft text

My interdisciplinary practice brings sculpture, sound, performance and interactive technologies into conversation with family archives and industrial landscapes. I build situations in which objects listen, accumulate traces and perform. In VRIM, audience and environmental sounds become generative musical sequences; my current MFA research at NSCAD University extends this attention to place through geolocated narratives about industrial pollution, illness and family history in Sydney, Nova Scotia. I ask how environmental harm persists in bodies and memories after official accounts declare a site restored, without reducing those inheritances to a single explanation.

At Est-Nord-Est, I propose to continue developing Creighton Stations: site-responsive sculptural listening devices that collect and replay fragments of their surroundings. Helen Creighton’s folkloric collection provides a starting point for questioning what recording technologies preserve, exclude or transform. Built from salvaged cabinetry, medical shipping boxes and found hardware, the stations house microphones, microcontrollers, storage and speakers. Sound-triggered recording and intermittent playback make each object both archive and performer. Image transfers and QR-linked spatial narratives connect its surfaces to further layers of sound, text and memory.

This work builds on my experience making underwater and shoreline recordings for Art Factory: Offshoots Art Project, developing location-aware interactive fiction, and combining archives with sculptural assemblage in The Wrong John Carroll. The residency would allow me to bring these approaches together through sustained material and acoustic experimentation. I would test how the stations respond to a place beyond the project’s existing Nova Scotia sites, without treating distinct local histories as interchangeable. Walking and listening would inform experiments with enclosures, recording thresholds and playback intervals. Studio tests would explore how multiple stations listen and respond to one another, asking when a device documents a place and when its behaviour changes what it records. My aim is a refined group of prototypes and a method for future site-specific installations, rather than a predetermined finished exhibition.

Recording involving participants would be developed through explicit invitation and consent, with clear information about recording and playback. Outdoor tests would depend on permission and suitable conditions; studio-based testing remains an alternative. I would bring the core electronics and software. Anticipated needs are a workbench, electricity, basic woodworking and assembly tools, and space for controlled sound tests, to be discussed with the residency. Specialist fabrication, equipment loans and outdoor installation access are not assumed.
