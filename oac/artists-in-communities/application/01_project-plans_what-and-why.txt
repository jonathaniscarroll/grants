--- NOTES (from rough draft, verbatim) ---

Developing a plan for the co-creation of a theatre work, in community, by theatre artist Robin Moir and visual artist/educator Sally Lawrence. The planning phase anticipates the production of an original musical theatre folk opera etc by Robin alongside two communities Sally lives and works in - The Forest Courtyard Initiative, a small rural alternative Waldorf-Inspired forest/farm school near Tottenham, north of Toronto; and Hesperus Fellowship, an independent living retirement community in Thornhill with a diverse social group where senior residents are invited to take initiative, explore, develop and share their gifts while cultivating meaningful relationships that is home to many artists, educators, and in Thornhill. Through the planning period, the artists (Sally and Robin) will develop the initial content to be presented to the community stakeholders. The implementation of the plan developed in this period will result in co-creating the work with the participants from both  the communities using a variety of creative methodologies, from devised theatre, to crafting, song writing circles, creative movement and other workshops.

The planning phase is crucial to lay the foundation for the co-creative process: Robin will continue developing a storyboard for the narrative, fleshing out characters and writing three to five initial musical numbers to establish the work's sonic aesthetic; Sally will build out a curriculum to guide participation, structuring the flow of co-creation for the eventual participants - as well as establishing visual motifs for the collaborative artistic production to follow. Both artists will maintain communication in the form of monthly meetings with the stakeholders at the retirement community and school - ensuring buy-in for the project beyond the planning phase.

The theatre piece itself is currently in the initial stages of conceptualization by Robin, as a work about Norse mythology, specifically focusing on the narratives of female characters from within the folklore. The piece builds off their previous production, A Song For Hephaestus, a folk opera which used Greek mythology as a lens through which to explore narratives about disability, injustice, and the liberatory potential of creative labour. Robin uses well known mythological characters and stories combined with rich songwriting to empower the participants in their work alongside audiences. In Spring/Summer 2025, Robin and Sally staged two productions of A Song For Hephaestus at Forest Courtyard Initiative and the Toronto Waldorf School, adapted for children aged 7 - 13. Following those successful productions, the two artists engaged in the co-creation of a musical theatre work that explored the stories of four major Abrahamic religions (Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Baha'i Faith) through original songs and popular music, structured into a narrative that was performed by children at the Forest Courtyard School.

The initiative that results from this plan, will build from those successful productions, with the goal of creating a new work that uses well-known mythological narratives to tell new stories that will resonate with both the youth at the school and elderly at the retirement community
