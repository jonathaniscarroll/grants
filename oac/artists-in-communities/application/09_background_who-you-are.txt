I am a multidisciplinary artist, musician, theatre creator and filmmaker. I also have experience working as an educator, guide, storyteller, etc
As a queer, neurodivergent person living with invisible disabilities... growing up in poverty in rural communities and being exposed to people of all walks of life, backgrounds, and income brackets as I travelled and grew as an artist, I've had a lot of experience inhabiting a variety of spaces, fitting myself into them like a chameleon yet never feeling quite like I belonged... finding spaces where each person in the community is lifted up through expression - not brought down by the weight of perfectionism, but given the tools to emancipate themselves through the process of creation
Exploring being othered, how it feels to be on the outside of societal norms - and seeing how every person experiences this to different degrees - whether through race, gender, sexuality, income inequality, disability, trauma, etc  - how our differences, what we do not understand about each other can always be a key into understanding more about the not only each other but also world around us and ourselves.
Deepening empathy through creative work
I am going to tell you a story: a lie made of truth - how can mythology and fiction explain simple truths to an audience, to a community, even to the creators of the work? How can a story or a piece of music put you into another person's experience, make you walk in their shoes?
Emancipatory propaganda art - how do we emancipate ourselves through the act of creation and make visible the propaganda all around us - how do we turn it into
Working with people - bringing out their potential and their super powers - what makes them unique, what only they can bring to a project to make it alive and magical
When we work in community, in collective creation, everyone is empowered and the art becomes more alive, more magic when each person's voice
