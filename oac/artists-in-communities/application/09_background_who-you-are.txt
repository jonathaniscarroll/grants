--- DRAFT ---

I am a multidisciplinary artist, musician, theatre creator and filmmaker, and I also work as an educator, guide and storyteller. My folk opera A Song for Hephaestus used Greek mythology to explore disability, injustice and the liberatory potential of creative labour, and my current work turns to Norse mythology.

I am queer and neurodivergent, I live with invisible disabilities, and I grew up in poverty in rural communities. As I travelled and grew as an artist I moved among people of every background and income bracket, fitting myself into each space like a chameleon yet never quite belonging. That experience of being on the outside of societal norms shapes what I make. I look for spaces where everyone is lifted up through expression, not weighed down by perfectionism, and given the tools to free themselves through the act of creating.

Everyone experiences being othered to some degree, through race, gender, sexuality, income, disability, trauma and more. What we do not understand about each other can be a way in to understanding each other, the world and ourselves. I use myth and fiction for that: a story can explain simple truths to an audience, a community and even the people making it, and a song can put you in someone else's shoes. I am interested in how creation can make the propaganda around us visible, and help people free themselves from it.

My strength is working with people. I love bringing out what is unique in each person, what only they can contribute to make a project alive. In collective creation, everyone is empowered and the work becomes more alive when each person's voice is in it.
