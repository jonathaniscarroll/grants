--- DRAFT ---

Sally Lawrence will be my mentor, the liaison between me and both organizations, and lead teacher for the Forest Courtyard children. That combination is convenient and, more importantly, rich for everyone involved. She can connect what we plan and create in the community to the children's education, so the work becomes part of their learning rather than an extra.

Immediate benefits, during this planning phase:
- Children: many of them were in the productions Sally and I led together at Forest Courtyard in May 2025 and June 2026, so the relationship is already established. This phase lets that relationship go deeper. They will meet Norse mythology in class, see the storyboard and songs as they develop, and be in conversation with the work from the start.
- Hesperus residents: they are invited to say how they want to take part, as leaders sharing a craft they know well or as learners picking up something new. Being asked, and having their knowledge treated as valuable, is a benefit in itself.
- Both communities: monthly meetings give each a real voice in shaping the project before any creation begins.
- Me, as the artist: mentorship from Sally, who has 45 years of experience as an artist, teacher and collaborator on large projects, and time to develop the opera carefully with the people it is for.

Ongoing benefits, as the work continues into a project phase:
- Children can be immersed in mythology, history and folklore, learn real-world skills, and see that their ideas can become something tangible that affects their community. That could be life-changing for them.
- Children gain elders to learn from. Guidance from Hesperus residents can offer new perspective and wisdom, and intergenerational wisdom-sharing and respect for older generations are sorely lacking in our society.
- Residents spend time with young children in mentorship roles, are invited to play and explore new ideas with younger generations, and are uplifted as community leaders.
- Sally has lived and worked among the Hesperus community for many years, and that trust carries forward and helps both communities connect.

Together, these benefits are about connection: a rural school community and a suburban retirement community, each with much to give the other, building a relationship that can last beyond any one project.
