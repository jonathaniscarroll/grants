SALLY LAWRENCE: bio, mentoring and liaison notes
(Point-form notes from rough draft, verbatim. Sally's own account; convert to a CV/bio of up to 1 page for upload.)

--- ARTISTIC HIGHLIGHTS ---
In 1985, Moved to Earlton with my partner to create a life-size moose and wolves sculpture carved out of cedar for the Northern Frontier Zoo
1987-Collaborated on a 10 metre high granite abstraction of a mine headframe with carved negative bas reliefs surrounded by 5 life-size figures of miners welded in mild steel and coated with bronze for the Miner's Memorial Foundation in Kirkland Lake. Completed in 1994
1996: Illustrated a book written by Chuck Angus and Britt Griffin, The Life, Death, and Life of a Mining Town. Archival photographs were collaged, painted and drawn to depict the passage of time
1998: Welded 2 life-size figures of Mr. Schumacher and Sandy McIntyre for the Schumacher Revitalization Committee
2000, collaborated on several bas relief sculptures in nickel-silver depicting miners from different historical eras for the Town of Cobalt. These sculptures were recently revitalized in 2023
2001: Welded 3 life-size nickel-silver figures of different ages, genders and ethnic backgrounds to depict the diversity of the Brampton and Mississauga District workforce with a solar sculpture and labyrinth to commemorate the Day of Mourning for workers killed or injured on the job. Completed in 2013.

--- MENTORING ---
I have much to offer as a mentor because of my 45 years working as an artist and teacher and my wealth of experience as a collaborator on large projects. I have collaborated on sculptures and murals and have worked on art and poetry projects with groups of students. I enjoy how the end result is always greater than the sum of its parts and how something magical happens when people join together to share and uncover their talents to create something bigger than themselves. The result is unpredictable and exciting because it evolves within the community it expresses. I have worked with Labour, Arts, and educational organizations on huge projects where timelines and budgets must be adhered to.

--- LIAISON ---
I was a professional artist who has taught a wide variety of ages of children and adults, so I welcome the role of liaison between the artist and school. Also, I have lived at Hesperus and have enjoyed its unique and vital senior community for 17 years. I look forward to working on a multi-generational arts project with the residents. Bringing elderly people together with children is an exciting notion that I believe would be mutually beneficial to both communities. Children will be encouraged to uncover blossoming talents and older people will have the opportunity to revisit or discover gifts that may be lying below the surface.
